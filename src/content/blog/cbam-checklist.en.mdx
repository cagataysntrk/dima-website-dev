The data needed for CBAM rarely lives in one system. Product identity may sit in ERP, supplier information in procurement records, energy consumption in manufacturing systems, and shipment information somewhere else.

The hard part is therefore not only calculating an emissions number. It is being able to show **which product, material, supplier, production period and shipment support that number**.

## Build the data chain first

A company should be able to answer:

- Which product and production period are in scope?
- Which materials were used?
- Which supplier and purchase records support those materials?
- What energy or fuel consumption belongs to the period?
- Which factor and calculation method were used?
- Which shipment or customer request does the result support?
- If source data changed later, who changed it and when?

Without that chain, a number may exist in a spreadsheet but remain difficult to reproduce or audit.

## What a useful checklist checks

A useful checklist asks more than whether a file exists.

1. Is product classification clear?
2. Is the production period and facility scope defined?
3. Do material and supplier records match?
4. Is the measurement source for energy and fuel known?
5. Is the factor and version recorded?
6. Can the result be linked to the relevant product and shipment?
7. Are missing or estimated inputs explicitly identified?
8. Can the same source chain be reached when the result is reproduced?

## How Dima approaches the problem

Dima should not invent a regulatory calculation. Its role is to connect company records so the origin of a result stays visible, detect missing links, and route the issue to the right person.

That turns compliance work from a one-off spreadsheet exercise into a record chain that can be inspected again when needed.

## Three management questions

Management should be able to see:

- Which products have insufficient data coverage?
- Which supplier or source is breaking the evidence chain?
- Which missing item becomes a reporting or customer-request risk if unresolved?

Decision intelligence becomes useful when it shows which gap matters to the business, not merely that a field is empty.
