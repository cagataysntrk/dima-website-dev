At month-end three records disagree: customer balances say one thing, bank movements another, and stock on the floor something else. The problem is often not that one record is simply wrong. It is that **the traces left by the same business event across several systems cannot be connected reliably**.

## All three records can be correct

Consider one sale:

- The ERP records the order and shipment.
- Accounting records the invoice and receivable.
- The bank records the collection later.
- Inventory records the stock movement.

These records belong to the same event, but they may be created at different times, under different identifiers and by different teams. Each screen can look correct in isolation while the company-level relationship is broken.

So before asking “which number is right?”, reconciliation often needs a more basic question:

**Do these records actually belong to the same customer, order, invoice and payment chain?**

## Why a fourth spreadsheet does not solve it

Copying differences into another workbook can create short-term visibility, but it does not make the relationship durable. The matching exercise starts again next month.

A stronger approach is to keep the source systems and establish the shared entities between them:

- customer,
- invoice,
- order,
- bank transaction,
- inventory movement,
- date and amount relationships.

The system can then investigate not only that two figures differ, but where in the transaction chain the difference appeared.

## How Dima approaches the problem

Dima is not designed to replace every system with one ERP. It connects existing sources inside the Company Brain context.

When a receivables discrepancy appears, for example:

1. The relevant customer and invoices are identified.
2. Due dates and payment history are compared.
3. Bank movements are searched for matched and unmatched collections.
4. Order and shipment relationships are checked.
5. The finding is presented together with evidence showing which records support it.

This turns reconciliation from “finding a difference in Excel” into an **entity and relationship investigation**.

Dima does not need to declare one record correct automatically. When ambiguity remains, it is often more valuable to keep that ambiguity visible, show what each source says and leave the decision-maker with an investigation that can be checked.
