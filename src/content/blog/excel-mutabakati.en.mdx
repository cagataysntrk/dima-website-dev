At month end, three records disagree: receivables show one balance, the bank another movement, and operational inventory tells a different story.

The problem is often not that one record is simply wrong. The traces left by the same business event in different systems cannot be connected.

## All three records can look correct

Consider one sale. The order and shipment exist in ERP, the invoice and receivable movement exist in accounting, collection appears later in the bank, and inventory changes elsewhere.

They belong to the same event but may be created at different times and under different keys.

The first reconciliation question is therefore not which number is correct. It is whether these records really belong to the same customer, order, invoice and payment chain.

## Why a fourth spreadsheet is not a durable fix

Copying differences into another spreadsheet creates temporary visibility but does not preserve the relationship.

A stronger approach keeps existing systems in place and establishes shared relationships across customer, invoice, order, bank movement, inventory movement, date and amount.

## How Dima approaches the problem

Dima is not intended to replace every source system with one ERP. It connects existing sources inside the Company Brain.

A representative investigation can identify relevant customers and invoices, compare due dates with payment history, separate matched and unmatched bank collections, inspect order and shipment relationships, and show which record chain explains the difference.

When uncertainty remains, preserving it and showing what each source says is more valuable than forcing a false certainty.

## The real output of reconciliation

A strong process should also explain why the difference appeared, whether the cause recurs, which process needs correction, who owns the fix, and whether the issue can be detected earlier next month.

That turns reconciliation into part of continuous financial control rather than month-end cleanup.
