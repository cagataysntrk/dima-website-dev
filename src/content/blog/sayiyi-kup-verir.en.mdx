When a manager asks why scrap rose last month, reaching the answer can take days.

There is a deeper problem: the company often depends on someone asking the right question before an important change is noticed.

Dima is designed to reverse that loop. It monitors company data and creates a finding when a meaningful deviation or opportunity appears.

For this to be trustworthy, one distinction is fundamental:

**The language model can help the investigation. It should not invent the number.**

## Where the numeric result comes from

Numeric authority stays in the defined analytical layer:

1. the business measure is tied to an explicit definition;
2. the query is planned and bounded before execution;
3. it runs against the real data source;
4. the result travels with source, period and entity context.

The language model may explain the result. It should not fabricate it.

## A finding is more than a number

A useful finding should investigate when the change started, which products or machines concentrate it, what normal behavior was, whether the pattern occurred before, and what financial or operational impact it creates.

Dima therefore does not stop at the KPI. It investigates cause candidates through related entities and relationships.

## Where conversation enters

Conversation does not have to be the starting point.

When a user opens a finding, the business domain, entities, period, evidence and active investigation are already known. Follow-up questions continue the same investigation.

## Why evidence belongs inside the product

A correct result is hard to defend if nobody can see where it came from.

Evidence should stay inside the finding: data source, period, related entities, calculation result and relationships used in the investigation.

Dima's goal is not to say that AI answered. The goal is to show why a finding deserves attention and which data supports it.
